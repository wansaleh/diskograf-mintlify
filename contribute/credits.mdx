Credits are the heart of Diskograf. They are what transforms a list of song titles into a genuine archive — connecting every song to the composers who wrote it, the producers who shaped it, and the performers who brought it to life. A song entry without credits is a starting point; a fully credited song is a piece of documented history.

## Credit Roles

Diskograf recognises four primary credit roles. Each maps to a distinct creative contribution:

- **Composer** — the person who wrote the music or melody of the song. On instrumental tracks, this may be the only writing credit.
- **Lyricist** — the person who wrote the song's lyrics. On songs with no words, this field will be empty.
- **Producer** — the person who oversaw the recording and production process. This may be a dedicated record producer, the performing artist, or both.
- **Performer** — the recording artist or artists who performed the song. This is usually the same as the main artist on the song entry, but may include featured artists or session performers.

One person can hold multiple roles on the same song. A singer-songwriter who writes their own words and melody is both the lyricist and the composer. An artist who self-produces their own record may appear as performer, composer, lyricist, and producer all at once. Diskograf handles this naturally — simply add the person under each role that applies.

## Adding a Credit

<Steps>
  <Step title="Navigate to the Song Page">
    Go to the page for the song you want to credit. You can find it by searching for the song title or artist name from the main search bar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the Credit Editor">
    On the song page, click **Edit credits** or the add credit button. This opens the credit editing interface where you can add new credits or modify existing ones.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the Credit Role">
    Choose the role you want to add — Composer, Lyricist, Producer, or Performer. You'll add one credit at a time, so if a person holds multiple roles, you'll go through this process once for each role.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Search for the Artist">
    Type the artist's name into the search field. Artists who already have credits elsewhere in the Diskograf catalogue appear first in the results, making it quick to find frequent collaborators and well-known figures. If the artist isn't in the system yet, you can create a new profile from within the credit editor without losing your place.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm and Save">
    Select the correct artist from the search results and save the credit. It will appear on the song page immediately.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Editing an Existing Credit

If a credit is wrong — the wrong person was attributed, a name is misspelled, or a role was assigned incorrectly — you can correct it directly from the song page.

Open the credit editor the same way you would to add a new credit, then locate the existing credit you want to change. You can update the attributed artist, correct the spelling of a name on their profile, or remove a credit that was added in error. Changes take effect immediately on the song page.

If the issue is with an artist's name rather than the attribution itself, update the name on the artist profile directly. Corrected artist names propagate to all song pages and catalogue cards that reference that artist.

## Credit Attribution Best Practices

Accurate credits depend on contributors doing the work to verify what they add. Before attributing a credit, check at least one of the following:

- The physical release — sleeve notes, inlay cards, or label text are the most authoritative source
- Official streaming metadata on platforms like Spotify or Apple Music
- The artist's or label's official website or social media
- A credible published source such as a music publication or academic record

When multiple sources conflict, note the discrepancy and default to the most primary source available — the physical release takes precedence over streaming metadata, for example.

<Warning>
  Do not add credits you cannot verify. Inaccurate credits undermine the integrity of the archive and can be difficult to untangle once they propagate. If you are unsure who held a particular role on a song, leave that credit field empty rather than guess. Someone with access to the right source material will be able to fill it in accurately.
</Warning>
