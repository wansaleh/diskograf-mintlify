Every edit you make to Diskograf is attributed to you and shown on your profile. Your contribution history is a running record of the work you have done on the catalogue, useful for tracking your own activity and for helping the community trust the data.

## Where to Find It

Sign in and open your profile at `diskograf.com/:your-username`. You will see a **Contributions** tab alongside your other profile sections. Anyone viewing your public profile can also see this history.

## What Gets Tracked

Diskograf records the meaningful edits you make to the catalogue, including:

- Songs you add.
- Credits you add, change, or remove.
- Artist profiles you create or update.
- Label information you add or correct.
- Genre tags you apply.

Each entry is timestamped and links back to the song, artist, or label you edited, so you can jump straight to what you changed.

## Reading the Timeline

Contributions are shown in reverse chronological order, newest first. Each entry shows:

- **What** was changed (for example, "added credit", "updated artist name", "added song").
- **Where** the change was made, as a link to the affected page.
- **When** the change happened.

You can page through older activity to see your full history back to the day you joined.

## Contributions on Other Profiles

Contribution history is public. Visiting another contributor's profile shows the same timeline for their activity, which is the primary way Diskograf keeps community edits accountable and visible.

If you want to see who has worked on a specific song, open that song's page and look at its contributor timeline. It shows every contributor who has touched the record and when.

## Reverting or Correcting a Change

Diskograf does not currently expose a one-click revert. If you notice a change (yours or someone else's) that needs to be corrected, edit the affected record directly and note the correction. For persistent disagreements, reach out via the [Contact](/support/contact) page.

<Note>
  Because contribution history is public and permanent, treat every edit as your byline. Care with data is what keeps the catalogue trustworthy.
</Note>
