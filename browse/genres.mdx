Genre is one of the most useful ways into a music catalogue. Whether you're tracing the arc of Malaysian pop, digging into rock, ballad, dangdut, or nasyid, Diskograf's genre pages give you a single place to see every song and artist associated with that sound.

## Finding Genres

Open the Genres index from the main navigation to see the full list of genres represented in the catalogue. Each entry shows the genre name and a cover image where one has been added.

Genres are also visible on every song and artist page. Clicking a genre tag anywhere in the catalogue takes you straight to that genre's page.

## What a Genre Page Shows

Each genre page collects everything Diskograf knows about that style of Malaysian music:

- **The catalogue of songs** tagged with that genre, paginated and sortable.
- **The artists** most closely associated with the genre, based on their credited work.
- **Related metadata** such as labels active in the space and notable releases.

Songs within a genre can be searched and filtered further using the same tools available across the rest of the site. See [Search](/browse/search) for the full filter syntax.

## Combining Genres with Other Filters

Genre filters combine cleanly with everything else. From the search bar:

```text
genre:pop artist:sheila decade:2000
```

Returns pop songs by an artist matching "sheila", released during the 2000s.

On a genre page itself, the built-in filters let you narrow to a specific role (composer, lyricist, producer), sort by year, and page through the catalogue.

## Missing or Incorrect Genres

Genres are contributed and refined by the community, just like every other credit in Diskograf. If a song is tagged incorrectly, or a genre is missing entries you'd expect to see, you can help by editing the song's credits. See [Adding Credits](/contribute/credits) for how genre tags fit into the song editor.

<Tip>
  Genre pages are a good starting point for research. They surface artists you may not have encountered under the same style label, and they make patterns across decades much easier to spot.
</Tip>
